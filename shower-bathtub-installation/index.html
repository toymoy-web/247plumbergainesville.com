Anti-scald valves and why they matter

Modern plumbing code requires showers to have a pressure-balancing or thermostatic mixing valve. It keeps the water temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet or starts the dishwasher, so the shower doesn't suddenly spike hot.

Many older Gainesville homes still have original two-handle valves without that protection. Replacing the valve during a remodel, or on its own, is one of the most practical safety upgrades in the house, especially for young children and older adults.

Hard water plays a part here too. Scale builds up in valve cartridges until the handle is stiff or the temperature control drifts. A water softener helps new valves last longer.

Older bathrooms in historic Gainesville homes

Bathrooms in pre-war homes in the Duckpond and University Heights often have original cast iron clawfoot tubs, galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains. Many owners want to keep the tub and upgrade what's behind the wall.

That usually means new supply lines to the tub filler and a check of the drain under the floor. If the supply lines throughout the house are the same age, pairing the bathroom project with full repiping saves opening walls twice.

How a tub-to-shower conversion works

  1. 1
    Plan the layout. Drain location, valve height, niche and bench positions are set before demolition.
  2. 2
    Remove the tub. The old tub is disconnected and taken out.
  3. 3
    Rework the drain. The drain is moved to the new shower pan location. On a slab, this means opening a section of concrete.
  4. 4
    Rough in the valve. The new valve is set at shower height with a showerhead riser.
  5. 5
    Pan, test and inspect. The shower pan is installed and flood tested before tile goes on.

A flood test catches pan leaks before they become a rotted subfloor or a stained ceiling below. We don't skip it.

Signs your shower valve needs attention

  • !
    Dripping from the showerhead after it's off. Worn cartridge or seals.
  • !
    Temperature swings when another fixture runs. No pressure balancing, or a failing cartridge.
  • !
    Handle is stiff or spins without shutting off. Scale or a broken stem.
  • !
    Water stains on the ceiling below the bathroom. A leak at the valve, the drain or the pan. Leak detection finds which.
