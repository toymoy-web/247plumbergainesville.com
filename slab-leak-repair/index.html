Slab Leak Detection and Repair in Gainesville, FL

A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or through a home's concrete foundation. Gainesville has two good reasons to expect them: many slab homes built from the 1960s through the 1980s with copper lines in the concrete, and ground underneath that moves.

We locate the leak electronically, show you where it is, and lay out the repair options side by side before any concrete is touched. The phone is answered 24/7 if water is coming up through the floor.

Slab leaks at a glance

  • Located by pressure test, acoustic and thermal methods first.
  • Direct repair, reroute or repipe, priced side by side.

Warm spot on the floor? Water coming up under the baseboards?

Homeowners rarely call asking for slab leak repair. They call about a symptom and do not know yet that it has a slab behind it. These are the ones that usually do.

  • !
    A warm or hot patch on tile or concrete. Hot water escaping under the slab heats the floor above it. Barefoot is often how people find it.
  • !
    Hissing or running water with every tap off. Pressurized water escaping through a pinhole makes a steady sound you can hear at night.
  • !
    A GRU meter that never stops. The low-flow dial keeps turning with the house shutoff open and every fixture closed.
  • !
    Damp carpet, cupping wood or lifting tile. Water wicks up through the concrete into flooring.
  • !
    The water heater runs constantly. It is refilling to replace hot water leaking out under the floor.
  • !
    New cracks in the slab or walls. Water washing out soil under the slab can let it settle.

Why Gainesville ground is hard on slab plumbing

Alachua County sits across the Cody Scarp, an old shoreline that divides two kinds of ground. On the southwest side, the Hawthorn clay layer has eroded away and the Ocala Limestone lies just a few feet under sand. Water moving through that limestone dissolves it slowly, and the soil above can settle unevenly. On the northeast side, a thicker clay blanket covers the rock instead.

Both are hard on pipe. Settling sand shifts the slab slightly over years and bends the copper inside it. Hawthorn clay swells when it gets wet and shrinks during dry spells, and Gainesville swings between both every year. A copper line cast into concrete cannot flex with either.

Pipe age finishes the job. Copper laid in the 1970s has spent 50 years carrying hard water at 8.2 grains per gallon. Where the line rubs against rebar, concrete or gravel, the wall wears thin and eventually pinholes.

Slab leak or sinkhole?

In Gainesville, cracks in a slab can raise a scarier question. Alachua County does have real sinkhole activity, including a sinkhole that opened near NW 13th Place in October 2020 and spread across four properties. Most slab cracks are nowhere near that serious.

A leaking water line is actually one of the more common local reasons soil washes out under a slab. If you see new cracks along with any sign of a leak, find the leak first. Fixing the water source stops the washout. If cracks keep growing after the leak is repaired, that is the point to bring in a geotechnical engineer.

For insurance, the distinction matters. Florida policies must cover catastrophic ground cover collapse, while broader sinkhole coverage is usually optional. A documented slab leak and a suspected sinkhole are handled very differently by your carrier.

How we find a slab leak

  1. 1
    Confirm and measure. A meter test confirms water is escaping and shows how fast.
  2. 2
    Split hot from cold. Each side is isolated and pressure tested, so we know which line is leaking before we search.
  3. 3
    Trace the line. Electronic line tracing shows the path each pipe takes through the slab.
  4. 4
    Locate the leak. Acoustic ground microphones listen along that path, and thermal imaging confirms hot-side leaks by the heat pattern.
  5. 5
    Mark and explain. The spot is marked on the floor and you get the options and prices before anything is cut.

If the problem might not be under the slab at all, general leak detection checks the yard line, attic runs and fixtures too.

Three ways to repair a slab leak

There is no single right answer. The best option depends on where the leak is, what the floor is made of and how old the rest of the pipe is.

Slab leak repair options compared.

Direct repair.A small section of floor is opened at the marked spot, the pipe is repaired and the concrete is patched.The leak is in an easy spot and the rest of the pipe is sound.Flooring at that spot has to be lifted and replaced.

On a first leak in a house with otherwise healthy pipe, direct repair is often the lowest-cost fix. On a second leak, we usually recommend a reroute or a full repipe, because the same pipe is under the same stress.

Reroutes and repipes are permitted work, inspected against the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

If water is coming up right now

Shut off the main valve where the supply line enters the house. If you cannot close it, a leaking slab line keeps running and keeps undermining the soil, so call us straight away.

If you know it is the hot side, turn off the water heater too, at the breaker for electric or the gas valve for gas. Then move rugs and furniture off the wet area and take photos for your insurer. Once the leak is found, the soaked flooring and drywall are a separate job for water damage restoration.

Hot-side leaks and cold-side leaks behave differently

A leak on the hot line is usually found first, because it announces itself. The floor gets warm, the water heater runs more than it should and the power or gas bill climbs along with the water bill.

A cold-side leak is quieter. The floor stays cool, so the first sign is often the GRU bill or a damp spot along a baseboard weeks later. It can run longer before anyone notices, which gives it more time to wash out soil under the slab.

The repair choice can differ too. Hot lines expand and contract every time the heater cycles, which adds wear, so a second hot-side leak is a strong argument for rerouting that line overhead.

After the repair: what to watch for

  • ✓
    Recheck the meter in a week. With everything off, the low-flow dial should stay still.
  • ✓
    Let the patch cure before flooring goes back. New concrete needs time to dry before tile or wood is laid over it.
  • ✓
    Watch the cracks you already had. Mark their ends with a pencil and a date. If they keep growing after the leak is fixed, call a structural or geotechnical engineer.
  • ✓
    Keep every document. Detection report, repair invoice and photos are what an insurer or a future buyer will ask for.

What changes the cost of a slab leak repair

  • $
    Repair method. Direct repair, reroute and repipe are priced very differently.
  • $
    Flooring at the leak. Tile, stone and hardwood cost more to open and restore than carpet.
  • $
    Leak location. Under a kitchen island or shower pan is harder to reach than an open floor.
  • $
    Hot or cold line. Hot-side leaks are often found faster thanks to thermal imaging.
  • $
    How much pipe is aging. A second leak on the same system changes the recommendation.
