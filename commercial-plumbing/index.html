Grease, the number one restaurant problem

Commercial kitchens produce grease faster than any home. Most of it should be caught by the grease interceptor or trap before it reaches the sewer. When that interceptor is overdue for pumping or its baffles are damaged, grease passes through and hardens in the line.

GRU runs an oil and grease management program for businesses on its sewer system. Keeping the interceptor maintained and the kitchen lines clear is part of staying compliant and keeping the doors open. Interceptor requirements themselves come from the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

For lines that have already built up, high-pressure hydro jetting after closing clears the grease without shutting down service. We can schedule it around your hours.

Backflow testing for commercial properties

GRU's cross-connection rules require backflow assemblies on non-residential properties to be maintained and tested every year, and GRU can require more frequent testing in some cases. A certified tester files the results with GRU.

Properties with fire sprinklers, irrigation, boilers or chemical equipment may have several assemblies. We keep a schedule so none of them slips. More detail is on our backflow testing page.

Leaks in multi-unit buildings

Apartment and student housing near the University of Florida often stacks units, so a leak upstairs shows up as a ceiling stain two floors down. Finding the source means testing fixtures and lines unit by unit.

Our leak detection process works well for this, since it isolates each line before anything is opened. Commercial buildings with several water heaters also get water heater repair service and replacement on a planned basis, so units are swapped before they fail during a busy week.
