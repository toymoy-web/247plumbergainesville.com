About Gainesville 24/7 Plumbing

Gainesville 24/7 Plumbing is a round-the-clock phone line and website for plumbing help in Gainesville and nearby Alachua County. Here's how it works, what you can expect when you call, and what we won't pretend to be.

Call (954) 697-4920, 24/7

How it works

When you call, you reach a line that's answered 24 hours a day, every day of the year. You describe the problem, and the call is handled by an independent local plumbing provider serving Gainesville and the surrounding towns. That provider does the work, sets the price and stands behind the job.

We say this plainly because it matters. The footer of every page carries the same disclosure: this site connects callers with independent providers and may be compensated for referrals. You deserve to know who you're dealing with before anyone comes to your door.

Emergencies and scheduled work

The same line handles both. At 2 a.m. it's usually a burst supply line, a backed-up main or a leaking water heater. During the day it's more often a water heater replacement quote, a repipe walkthrough or a drain that's been slow for a week.

Emergencies get the first available provider. Planned work is scheduled at a time that suits you, with a free estimate first, so you can compare before you commit.

What you can expect on every call

  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓

Why the pages on this site are so specific

Plumbing in Gainesville has its own quirks. The water from GRU runs about 8.2 grains per gallon hard, which wears out water heaters and faucet parts early. Much of the west side sits on sand over shallow limestone, and slab homes from the 1960s through the 1980s see more under-slab leaks. The historic districts near downtown still carry galvanized supply pipe and clay or cast iron drains.

Outside the city, the picture changes again. Alachua, Newberry, High Springs, Archer and Hawthorne each run on a different mix of city utilities, private wells and septic systems. We researched each one so the advice on this site fits the house you actually live in. The service areas page has the details.

Every page cites where its local facts come from: GRU, the city utilities, Alachua County, state agencies and published standards such as the Florida Building Code.

What we don't claim

You won't find invented reviews, star ratings, customer counts or years-in-business figures on this site. We don't show license numbers or certifications that haven't been confirmed. If that information is added, it will be real and checkable.

In the meantime, we encourage you to do what you should do with any plumber: ask for their Florida license number before work starts, and look it up on the state's Department of Business and Professional Regulation license search.

Questions worth asking any plumber, including us

Whoever you hire, these questions protect you. A good plumber answers all of them without hesitation.

  • ✓
    What's your Florida license number? Then check it on the DBPR license search. It takes a minute.
  • ✓
    Will this job need a permit? Water heater replacements, repipes, gas lines and sewer replacements usually do in Gainesville and Alachua County. Skipping one can cause trouble when you sell.
  • ✓
    Can I have the price before you start? For any job, you should know the cost before work begins, not after.
  • ✓
    What exactly failed, and can I see it? The old part, a photo or the camera video. Understanding the cause helps you prevent the next one.
  • ✓
    Is there a cheaper fix that would work? Sometimes a repair makes more sense than a replacement. Ask for both options.
  • ✓
    What does the warranty cover, and for how long? Get it in writing, and know who stands behind it.

These are the same questions we'd want answered for our own homes. If any of them gets a vague answer, that tells you something.

What falls outside plumbing

Some problems start with plumbing but end in another trade. Pool shell cracks and resurfacing belong to a pool contractor. Structural cracks that keep growing after a leak is fixed belong to a structural or geotechnical engineer. Gas leaks go to 911 and the gas provider first.

Water damage cleanup and drying are handled through a separate dedicated line, listed on the professional water damage restoration page. For everything else plumbing, the full list is on our plumbing services page.
