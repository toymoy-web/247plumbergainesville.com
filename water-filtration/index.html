Water Filtration Installation in Gainesville, FL

Water filtration installation puts a filter system on your home's water, either at the main line for the whole house or at the kitchen tap for drinking water. The right system depends on what's actually in your water, which is different for GRU customers than for homes on private wells.

Filtration is planned work. If a filter housing cracks and floods the garage, the line is answered 24/7.

Filtration at a glance

  • Matched to GRU's water report or a well water test.
  • Whole-house carbon, sediment and reverse osmosis.
  • Bypass valves so filters can be changed easily.
  • Pairs with a softener where hardness is the issue.

What's in Gainesville tap water

GRU's water comes from the Floridan aquifer and is treated at the Murphree plant with lime softening, recarbonation, filtration, fluoridation and disinfection. GRU's recent annual water quality reports list no drinking water violations, and the state rates the source wells as low risk.

So why filter? Mostly taste, smell and preference. Chlorine from disinfection gives tap water a taste some people dislike.

Disinfection also forms low levels of byproducts, which are regulated and tested. Some households simply prefer to reduce them further at the tap.

A filter is not a fix for hardness. At 8.2 grains per gallon, scale on fixtures is a job for a salt-based water softener, often paired with a filter.

Homes on private wells, toward Monteocha or west of Jonesville, need a different filter from homes on GRU water in neighborhoods like Haile Plantation or the Duckpond, because well water hasn't been treated at all. A water test tells you which problem you're actually solving.

Choosing a filter: start with these questions

  • ✓
    City water or well? GRU water and well water have very different problems.
  • ✓
    What bothers you? Taste, smell, stains, scale or a specific contaminant each point to a different system.
  • ✓
    Whole house or just drinking water? A whole-house system treats showers and laundry too. An under-sink unit treats what you drink and cook with.
  • ✓
    Do you want fluoride kept or removed? Carbon filters generally leave it. Reverse osmosis removes most of it.
  • ✓
    Is there a water test? Always, for wells. For GRU customers, the annual water quality report is the starting point.

Well water in rural Alachua County

Private wells in the county draw from the same limestone aquifer as GRU, but without a treatment plant in between. Common complaints are hardness, iron that stains fixtures orange, and a sulfur smell from hydrogen sulfide. Bacteria testing matters too, especially after flooding.

A well system usually needs a test first, then a sequence: sediment filter, iron or sulfur treatment, a softener if the water is hard, and sometimes disinfection. Homes around High Springs and other rural areas can need several of those steps. If the pump or pressure tank is struggling, well pump repair comes first.

What installation includes

  1. 1
    Review the water. GRU's report or a well test, plus what you want to change.
  2. 2
    Size the system. Flow rate matters. An undersized whole-house filter lowers pressure at every tap.
  3. 3
    Install with a bypass. Filters can be changed or serviced without shutting off the house.
  4. 4
    Connect drains where needed. Reverse osmosis and backwashing filters need a proper drain connection.
  5. 5
    Flush and test. New media is flushed, and fittings are checked for leaks.

Filters and the rest of your plumbing

A sediment filter ahead of a softener or a tankless water heater protects them from grit, which is especially useful on wells. A reverse osmosis unit needs a spare connection under the sink, and older, stuck shutoff valves are often replaced at the same time as part of faucet and sink repair.
