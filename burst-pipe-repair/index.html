Burst Pipe Repair · Gainesville, FL

Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Gainesville, FL

Burst pipe repair stops the water and replaces the failed section with new pipe. Then it finds out why the pipe failed, so it doesn't happen again a few feet down the line. In Gainesville, the cause is usually one of three things: a rare hard freeze, pressure that is too high, or pipe that has simply aged out.

A burst pipe is a true emergency. The line is answered 24/7, and the first question we'll ask is whether you've been able to shut off the water.

Burst pipe repair at a glance

  • Water stopped first, repair second.
  • Attic, wall, garage, exterior and yard lines.
  • Pressure checked so the next weak spot doesn't go too.
  • Answered 24/7, holidays included.

Water pouring out? Do these four things first

  1. 1
    Close the main shutoff. It's usually where the supply enters the house. If it won't turn, tell us on the phone and we'll help you find another way to stop the water.
  2. 2
    Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house. It drains the pressure out of the lines so the break slows to a drip.
  3. 3
    Turn off power to anything getting wet. If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, switch off those breakers, but only if you can reach the panel from a dry spot.
  4. 4
    Photograph the damage before moving things. Your insurer will want to see what happened before cleanup started.

Emergency burst pipe repair: tell the dispatcher these three things

Burst pipe calls come in at every hour, and the line answers every one. Most are cracked fittings, split supply lines or old galvanized and copper pipe that finally gave way, not frozen pipe.

The dispatcher will ask where the water is, whether the main is closed and whether it is coming through a wall or ceiling. If water is near outlets or light fixtures, say that first.

What to tell the dispatcher

  • Where the water is coming from, and how fast.
  • Whether the main shutoff is closed.
  • Whether water is near electrical outlets, a panel or light fixtures.

How pipes burst in a city that rarely freezes

Freezes are the obvious cause, and they do happen here. Some winters bring overnight lows below freezing, sometimes as late as March. Early 2026 brought record-breaking cold to much of Florida, and burst pipes followed across the state. Houses built for Florida weather often have supply lines in unheated attics, exterior walls and garages, which is exactly where they freeze.

Pressure is the quieter cause. When a home's pressure-reducing valve fails, pressure can climb well above what household fixtures and hoses were built for. Old or weak pipe gives way at the worst joint.

Age is the third. Polybutylene splits at its fittings, galvanized rusts through from the inside, and CPVC in a hot attic grows brittle over the years. None of them needs a freeze to fail.

The pipes most exposed to a freeze are in older raised homes, the kind found in the Duckpond and the Pleasant Street district, where supply lines run through a ventilated crawlspace. On newer slab homes in Haile Plantation or Tioga, the weak spots are hose bibs, irrigation backflow assemblies and pipes inside exterior garage walls.

Before the next cold night

When the forecast calls for temperatures in the 20s for several hours, these steps are worth ten minutes of your evening.

  • ✓
    Let indoor faucets on outside walls run a thin stream. Moving water resists freezing.
  • ✓
    Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls. Warm room air reaches the pipes.
  • ✓
    Disconnect garden hoses and cover the hose bibs. A hose holds water against the valve and freezes it.
  • ✓
    Wrap exposed pipes in the garage and at the well. Foam pipe sleeves are cheap. Towels work in a pinch.
  • ✓
    Know where your main shutoff is. Finding it at 3 a.m. with water running is much harder.

Insurance and the water left behind

Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe. They usually don't cover the pipe itself, and they may deny slow leaks that went on for weeks. The distinction is timing, so document the date and time you found it.

We fix the pipe. Drying out soaked drywall, insulation and flooring is a separate job, handled through our structural water damage restoration service, which runs on its own phone line. Starting the drying quickly limits mold in Gainesville's humidity.

When one burst means more to come

A single break from a hard freeze is bad luck. A second break in polybutylene or galvanized pipe within a year is a pattern. At that point, repiping the supply lines usually costs less than the next few emergencies.

Homes on private wells outside the city, around Hawthorne and other rural parts of the county, have pump and pressure-tank piping that sits exposed and freezes first. Insulating it is the cheapest fix there is. If water is missing after a repair, acoustic leak detection checks for a second break you can't see.

Cracked fittings, crawlspace pipes and supply valves

Callers describe a cracked elbow, a split PVC connector, a burst galvanized pipe or a valve that failed and is leaking through a wall. Each is a different repair, but each starts with stopping the water.

Under-house plumbing needs its own attention. Pipes in a crawlspace can leak for a long time before anyone hears them, and a sound like a shower running under the floor is a reason to close the main and call.
