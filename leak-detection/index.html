Leak Detection · Gainesville, FL

Emergency Leak Detection in Gainesville, FL

Leak detection is the process of finding where water escapes a plumbing system without tearing the house apart to look. In Gainesville, most of these calls start the same way: a GRU bill that jumped for no reason, a meter that keeps turning with every tap closed, or a damp spot nobody can explain.

We trace the leak with listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure tests, then mark the spot before anything gets cut. The phone is answered 24/7 if water is showing up right now.

Leak detection at a glance

  • Pinpoints the leak before walls, floors or yards are opened.
  • Covers supply lines, slabs, yard service lines and irrigation.
  • Written findings you can send to GRU for a leak adjustment.
  • Phone answered 24/7 for active leaks.

Meter spinning with every tap turned off?

That single symptom brings in more leak detection calls than any other. You can confirm it yourself in about ten minutes before you call anyone.

  • ✓
    Turn off every fixture and appliance. That includes the ice maker, the irrigation controller and any water softener that might be mid-cycle.
  • ✓
    Find the GRU meter box. It is usually near the street or sidewalk in a plastic or concrete box with a lid.
  • ✓
    Watch the low-flow indicator. Most meters have a small dial or triangle that spins with even a trickle. If it moves, water is leaving somewhere.
  • ✓
    Close the house shutoff and look again. If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the buried line between the meter and the house.

Write down what you saw. That one test cuts the search area roughly in half before a plumber arrives.

Emergency leak detection: when a leak cannot wait until tomorrow

Some leaks are an appointment. Others are an emergency, like a meter running fast with every tap off, water coming through a ceiling, or a wet patch spreading across the yard. For those, emergency leak detection means a technician at the property tonight with the locating gear, not a callback in two days.

Tell the dispatcher what you can see and hear, and whether you have already closed the main. If shutting the main stops the problem, the leak is on the house side of the valve. That one fact narrows the search before the truck leaves.

Until the technician arrives

  1. Close the main shutoff if water is damaging anything.
  2. Keep clear of outlets and the panel if water is nearby, and switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the breaker dry.
  3. Photograph the meter dial and any damage, since both help later with a GRU adjustment request.
  4. Leave walls and floors closed so the leak can be traced without guessing.

Why a hidden leak costs Gainesville homeowners twice

GRU does not meter wastewater separately. Sewer charges are calculated from the water that passes through your meter each month. So a leak that pours clean water into the ground still shows up on the wastewater side of the bill.

A running toilet or a pinhole under the slab can run for weeks before the bill arrives. By then you have paid for the water twice. Finding it fast matters more here than in towns that bill sewer at a flat rate.

There is some relief available. GRU offers courtesy leak adjustments when a leak is verified and the repair is documented, and the credit is applied only after usage drops back to normal. Keep the detection findings and the repair invoice together. Those two papers are what the adjustment request needs.

High-bill calls come from every part of town, but irrigation leaks dominate in planned neighborhoods like Haile Plantation and Tioga, where nearly every yard has in-ground sprinklers. In older homes near the Duckpond, the culprit is more often a corroded line under the floor.

How we find the leak without guessing

No single tool finds every leak. The method depends on what the pipe is made of, how deep it sits and whether the water is hot or cold.

Leak detection methods and where each one works best in Gainesville homes.
MethodHow it worksBest for
Pressure isolation.Sections of the system are shut off and held under pressure to see which one drops.Narrowing a leak to the hot side, cold side or yard line.
Acoustic listening.Ground microphones pick up the hiss of water escaping a pressurized pipe.Pipes under concrete slabs and buried yard lines.

Most jobs use two or three of these in sequence. The pressure test tells us which line, and the listening or thermal work tells us where on that line.

Where leaks tend to hide in Gainesville houses

Location follows construction. Slab homes built from the 1960s through the 1980s often run copper supply lines through the concrete. Those lines sit on sand and limestone that shifts, and the pipe wall thins over decades. When a leak turns out to be under the floor, our targeted slab leak repair team takes it from there.

Newer houses in the west and southwest often route supply lines through the attic instead. A leak up there shows up as a ceiling stain in a hallway or closet, usually after a hot stretch of summer.

Yard lines are the third common spot. A crack between the meter and the house never shows up indoors at all. It just keeps the meter moving, and that is a job for water service line repair rather than anything inside.

Other signs worth a leak test

  • !
    A warm spot on tile or concrete. Hot water escaping under a slab heats the floor above it.
  • !
    The water heater runs when nobody is using hot water. It is refilling a tank that is draining somewhere.
  • !
    Musty smell under carpet or behind a vanity. Humidity in Gainesville hides small leaks until mold gives them away.
  • !
    Low pressure at one end of the house only. Water is escaping upstream of those fixtures.
  • !
    Soggy grass in a dry week. A buried line or an irrigation zone is leaking.
  • !
    A toilet that refills on its own. Often a worn flapper. A quick simple toilet repair may end the mystery before a full leak test is needed.

What a leak detection visit looks like

  1. 1
    Meter and fixture check. We confirm the leak is real and measure how fast the meter moves.
  2. 2
    Isolation. Hot, cold, yard and irrigation lines are tested one at a time.
  3. 3
    Locating. Listening, thermal or tracer gas work narrows the leak to a spot we can mark on the floor or ground.
  4. 4
    Findings and options. You get the location, the likely cause and the repair choices with prices, before any repair starts.

If the house has several old leaks rather than one, patching can stop making sense. In that case we will say so and explain what repiping the supply lines would involve instead.

Paperwork for a GRU leak adjustment

If you plan to ask GRU for a courtesy adjustment, start collecting proof the day you notice the problem. It is much harder to rebuild afterward.

  • ✓
    Photograph the meter. Take a photo of the reading and the moving low-flow dial, with the date, before anything is repaired.
  • ✓
    Keep the detection findings. The written location and cause of the leak are the verification part of the request.
  • ✓
    Keep the repair invoice. It should name the address, the date and what was fixed.
  • ✓
    Wait for a normal bill. GRU applies adjustments after usage returns to normal, so the next reading matters too.

We write the findings in plain language with the location marked, so they can go straight into the request.

Leak detection for rentals and commercial buildings

Leaks in multi-unit buildings are harder to pin to one unit. A leak can start in an upstairs bathroom and show up two rooms away on the floor below. Isolation testing one unit at a time is slower, but it is the only way to bill the right repair to the right place.

Commercial properties add backflow assemblies, larger meters and long service lines, often with several buildings on one meter. Restaurants and offices near downtown and along Archer Road are typical. We schedule testing around business hours where we can, and after hours when the water can only be shut off at night.

Questions callers ask before booking a leak test

Do you have equipment that can see inside walls or under a slab? Yes. We match the tool to the pipe, because a plastic line buried in the yard needs a different approach than copper inside a wall. Listening gear, thermal imaging, pressure tests and tracer gas each have a job.

Will you fix it too? Many callers only want the leak marked so they can repair it themselves, and that is a fine outcome. If you want the repair as well, the same visit can continue once you approve the price.

If the house is for sale or an inspector flagged moisture, say so on the call. We can locate and document the leak so the repair and the paperwork match.

Water through a ceiling or inside a wall

A stain on a ceiling is rarely directly under the leak. Water travels along framing before it drops, so the source is often in the bathroom above, a supply line in the wall or a drain that has loosened.

We trace the line that feeds that area, test it under pressure and use thermal imaging to follow the moisture back. The goal is one small opening in the right place instead of several in the wrong ones.

Irrigation and yard lines that spin the meter

Callers often tell us the only wet thing on the property is the sprinklers, or that the irrigation valve runs fast when it opens. A zone valve, a broken head or a buried pipe can use thousands of gallons without leaving a puddle indoors.

We isolate one zone at a time until the meter stops. Pool, hot tub and water softener plumbing get the same treatment, since each can quietly keep water moving.
