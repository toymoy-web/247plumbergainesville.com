Pleasant Street · Gainesville, FL

Plumber in Pleasant Street, Gainesville, FL

Pleasant Street is a historic residential neighborhood just northwest of downtown Gainesville. The City of Gainesville calls it the oldest predominantly African-American residential area in the city, with wood frame vernacular houses, bungalows and two Romanesque churches.

Compact houses, porches and households of different sizes make for steady day to day plumbing needs. Call (954) 697-4920 to talk through a fixture, a water heater or a leak.

Where Pleasant Street is

Pleasant Street lies northwest of downtown. The National Register listing summary describes the district as running roughly between NW 2nd and NW 8th Avenues and NW 1st and NW 6th Streets. Main Street is used locally as a dividing line for school zones.

We refer to the area by those anchor streets, because neighbors may draw the edges a little differently.

Small houses, constant use

Many Pleasant Street homes are modest bungalows and cottages. Local listing data puts the typical single-family house at about 1,250 square feet. Smaller homes often have one main bathroom and one water heater serving everyone in the house.

That concentrates wear on a few fixtures. Toilets that run, faucets that drip and water heaters that struggle are the everyday calls. Toilet repair, faucet and sink repair and water heater repair are usually quick to diagnose and fix.

For owners and landlords of small houses

  • ✓
    Replace flexible supply lines. The braided hoses under sinks and behind toilets wear out. Swap them before they fail.
  • ✓
    Know your shutoffs. Find the main shutoff and the valves at each fixture so anyone in the house can stop a leak.
  • ✓
    Note your water heater age. The date is on the label. Planning a replacement is easier than an unplanned one.
  • ✓
    Report drips early. A small leak in a rental can turn into a larger repair if it goes unreported.

A neighborhood with a long history

Pleasant Street grew as a center of Black life in Gainesville, and landmarks such as Mount Pleasant Methodist Episcopal Church and the Dunbar Hotel are part of that story. Many families have lived on these blocks for generations, and the neighborhood has also seen new housing and new residents in recent years.

Whatever the age of your house, we start with what is actually in front of us and not with assumptions about the pipe.

Around Pleasant Street

Pleasant Street is covered along with the rest of Gainesville. Toward the university, University Heights is another historic residential area, and Duckpond is a short trip across downtown.
