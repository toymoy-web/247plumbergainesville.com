New construction, new plumbing questions

A brand-new house doesn't mean a problem-free one. The most common calls from newer Newberry homes are irrigation leaks, a water bill that seems too high for the household, water heaters that don't keep up with a growing family, and fittings that were never quite tight from the start.

Many newer homes run PEX supply lines through the attic rather than copper through the slab. That makes slab leaks less common, but a fitting that lets go overhead shows up as a ceiling stain.

If the bill jumps and you can't see water anywhere, professional leak detection checks the house, the irrigation zones and the buried service line one at a time.

City water, county wells and the septic transition

Inside city limits, the City of Newberry provides water and wastewater, and electric in part of the city. Outside those limits, many homes are on private wells and septic tanks.

That line is moving. The city is building a regional wastewater treatment plant intended to take about 600 septic tanks in neighboring communities offline, and it's adding a new water storage tower on State Road 26. When sewer reaches a property, the plumbing side of the switch includes running a new line from the house to the connection and abandoning the old tank properly.

Until then, septic homes around Newberry still need regular pumping. Our septic service covers that and the line from the house to the tank.

Before we arrive at your Newberry home

If water is running where it shouldn't, these four steps buy time. They are written for the newer houses that make up much of Newberry.

1

Find the shutoff the builder left.

Newer Newberry homes usually have a main valve on an outside wall or in the garage where the line enters, plus the meter valve at the street. Close the house valve first, then turn off the irrigation controller, since a split zone line can keep running on schedule.

2

Kill power to anything wet.

Trip breakers feeding wet rooms and shut off an electric water heater at its breaker. A tankless unit in the garage has its own breaker or plug, and it should be off before water reaches it.

3

Get belongings off new flooring.

Vinyl plank and carpet pad in newer builds soak up water fast. Pull rugs and boxes clear, and push standing water toward a door so it stops wicking under baseboards.

4

Call before it spreads.

Ring (954) 697-4920 at any hour and mention whether the house is on city water or a septic system. Newberry emergency calls, the Jonesville side included, are dispatched around the clock.
