Two kinds of Archer homes

Inside city limits, Archer's Public Works department runs the water plants, mains and roughly 570 water meters. That's a small system, and its crew is on call around the clock for problems on the city side of the meter.

Outside town, many homes rely on their own well, pump and pressure tank, and a septic system. When the pump stops, the house has no water at all, and there's no utility to call. That makes a dead well pump the most urgent plumbing problem out here.

The first question on the phone is always which kind of home you have. It decides whether we're looking at a service line, a well system or a septic tank.

Before we arrive: burst pipes and pump house leaks

Outside town, most Archer homes run on a private well, and that changes how you stop a leak. Here is what to do first.

1

Turn off the pump, not just a valve.

On a well, shut the breaker marked for the pump, then close the valve after the pressure tank if you can reach it safely. In town on city water, the main sits at the meter box by the street.

2

Kill power before you step in.

A pump house flooded by a burst fitting has 240-volt wiring close to the ground. Switch that breaker off at the house panel before you walk inside.

3

Move what the water can reach.

Lift feed, tools and stored goods off the floor of a barn or utility room, and keep people out until you know the power is off.

4

Call the line, any hour.

Call (954) 697-4920 day or night. Mention the pump size if you know it and whether the pressure tank still holds. Archer calls, farms off SR 24 included, are dispatched around the clock.

Pump houses and the occasional hard freeze

Rural Archer homes often have well piping and a pressure tank in a small pump house or in the open. On the rare nights the temperature drops well below freezing, that exposed piping is the first thing to split.

Wrapping exposed pipe, adding a small heat source to the pump house, or simply letting a faucet drip overnight prevents most freeze damage. If a line has already split, burst pipe repair covers it, pump house included.

Keeping a well and septic home running

  • ✓
    Watch the pressure gauge. A pump that short-cycles means the pressure tank needs attention.
  • ✓
    Put a surge protector on the pump circuit. Summer lightning is a common reason well controls fail.
  • ✓
    Pump the septic tank on schedule. Every three to five years for most households.
  • ✓
    Test the well water. Hardness and iron are common, and a water softener or filter handles what the test finds.

Septic tanks and the line from the house are covered by septic service.
