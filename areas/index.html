Plumbing Service Areas Around Gainesville, FL

Gainesville 24/7 Plumbing covers all of Gainesville, the Haile Plantation community and six nearby towns in Alachua County. Each one runs on a different mix of city water, private wells, sewer and septic, and that changes the plumbing problems people call about. The pages below cover what's specific to each place.

Areas we cover

Nearby towns and communities

Haile Plantation, 32608

A 2,600-household planned community on GRU water, with tankless heaters and irrigation on most lots.

Micanopy, 32667

The county's oldest town, south across Paynes Prairie, with septic and private wells across its wide ZIP.

Alachua, 32615

City-run water and sewer, a new water facility after Irma, and Progress Park businesses.

Newberry, 32669

Fast-growing, new-build homes on larger lots, and a regional sewer plant replacing septic tanks.

High Springs, 32643

Historic downtown near the Santa Fe River, mid-way through a septic-to-sewer conversion.

Archer, 32618

A small railroad town on city water, with private wells and septic on the rural lots around it.

Hawthorne, 32640

Between two lakes on the clay side of the county, with its own city water and sewer.

Gainesville

Every residential ZIP in the city, on GRU water and sewer.

Gainesville coverage

In Gainesville itself we cover every residential ZIP: 32601, 32603, 32605, 32606, 32607, 32608, 32609, 32641 and 32653. That runs from the Duckpond and the other historic districts near downtown to the Millhopper area, the Tower Road corridor and east Gainesville. Water and sewer there come from GRU.

Southwest of the city, Haile Plantation is unincorporated but shares GRU utilities and Gainesville addresses. Smaller communities like Jonesville, Tioga, Monteocha, Waldo and La Crosse don't have their own pages, but they're covered on the same 24/7 line.

The main Gainesville emergency plumber page covers the city in detail, including why slab leaks run high on the west side and how GRU's hard water affects water heaters.

How coverage works

Every town gets the same 24/7 phone line and the same approach: a price before any work starts, and the problem explained in plain language. What changes is the kind of problem.

Homes on private wells and septic, common on rural lots across the county, need a different set of skills from homes on city water and sewer. We ask which you have on the first call, so the right equipment comes on the truck.
