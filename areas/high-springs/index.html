The springs shape the plumbing here

High Springs sits in karst country. Water moves quickly through the limestone below the town, which is what feeds the springs along the Santa Fe River. It also means nitrogen from septic systems reaches the groundwater and the springs quickly.

For years most residents here relied on septic tanks. The city has been working to move homes onto sewer, starting with the Hornsby Springs project and continuing in stages, with help from the Suwannee River Water Management District. As of 2021 the city served roughly 1,700 sewer connections, with a goal of serving all 2,800. In 2023 it won a federal grant to extend a wastewater line about three miles along CR 236 to I-75.

So High Springs homes fall into two groups: on sewer, or still on septic and likely to face a connection decision. Both need plumbing help, just different kinds.

Downtown High Springs and its older pipes

The High Springs Historic District is listed on the National Register, with more than 200 contributing buildings from the town's railroad and phosphate era. Homes and shops that old often still carry original cast iron drain lines and older supply pipe.

Those lines tend to fail slowly: a clog that keeps coming back, a sewer smell in the yard, or rust in the water. A camera inspection before any major work shows whether the line needs a repair or a replacement, and keeps repair openings small in a historic building.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in High Springs

High Springs homes split between city water, private wells, septic and the new sewer. The first steps depend on which one your house runs on.

1

Shut the water, city or well.

On city water, close the main at the meter box or where the line enters the house. On a well outside town, switch off the pump breaker in the panel or pump house, which stops the pump from feeding the break.

2

Power off near the water.

Turn off breakers for flooded rooms. In older downtown homes with mixed wiring, don't reach into standing water to unplug anything. Flip the breaker instead.

3

Stop using drains on septic.

If the problem is a backup rather than a leak, stop all water use. On septic, every flush adds to a tank that can't take it, so keep kids and pets away from any sewage.

4

Call our 24/7 line.

(954) 697-4920 is answered all night. Tell us whether you are on septic or the new city sewer, because that decides whether the fix starts at the tank or the lateral.

Protecting a well and septic home near the springs

  • ✓
    Pump the septic tank on schedule. Every three to five years for most households, per EPA guidance.
  • ✓
    Keep the drain field clear. No parking, no new trees, no additions over it.
  • ✓
    Test the well after flooding. Heavy rain in karst country can move surface water into groundwater quickly.
  • ✓
    Fix running toilets fast. Extra water overloads the drain field and pushes more into the ground.

If your home is on a private well, submersible well pump repair covers the pump, tank and controls, and water filtration handles what a water test turns up.
