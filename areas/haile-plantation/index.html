From the Village Center to the cul-de-sacs off the Haile Loop, a burst line or a dead water heater gets the same 24/7 emergency line as the rest of Gainesville.

A planned community built in phases

Haile Plantation was laid out as a New Urbanist development, with a walkable village center and neighborhoods added in phases over several decades. Homes for sale today range from around 1990 builds to custom homes finished in the 2010s.

That spread matters for plumbing. A 1990 house may be on its third water heater and still have original supply lines, while a 2014 parade home was built with a tankless unit and spray foam insulation. We ask the year built on the first call, because it points to where the problem is likely to be.

What Haile homes call about

Most calls here fall into three groups. The first is water heaters, both aging tanks in garages and tankless units that have scaled up on GRU's hard water. A tankless heater that shuts off mid-shower is usually telling you it needs descaling, not replacement.

The second is irrigation. Nearly every yard has an in-ground system, and a cracked lateral or a failed backflow assembly can run unnoticed for weeks in sandy soil. The GRU bill is often the first sign.

The third is drains and sewer laterals under wooded lots. Many Haile streets sit under a canopy of live oaks, and their roots find the joints in older sewer lines.

Water where it shouldn't be in Haile? First steps

Haile homes share a few features that change the first steps in an emergency. These fit most houses and townhomes in the community.

1

Close the house valve, then the meter.

Most Haile homes have a main shutoff where the line enters the house, often in the garage or on an outside wall. If you can't find it, the GRU meter box near the street has a valve too.

2

Turn off the irrigation controller.

A split irrigation line will run again on its next scheduled cycle. Set the controller to off so it can't add water while you wait.

3

Kill power or gas to the water heater.

For a tank, switch off the breaker or set the gas valve to pilot. For a tankless unit, shut off its breaker and close the cold supply valve underneath.

4

Call the 24/7 line.

Call (954) 697-4920 and give your street and the nearest Haile landmark, like the Village Center or Market Square. Emergency dispatch runs around the clock.
