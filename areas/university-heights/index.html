University Heights · Gainesville, FL

Plumber in University Heights, Gainesville, FL

University Heights is a pair of local historic districts, North and South, in the area between the University of Florida and downtown Gainesville. The City of Gainesville says the North district reflects styles from the 1920s to the early 1950s and the South district reflects mid-century suburban growth.

Many houses here are owner occupied and many are rentals. Call (954) 697-4920 to talk through a drain, a toilet or a repair that needs to be coordinated with a landlord.

University Heights at a glance

  • North and South historic districts between UF and downtown.
  • North includes the University Terrace and Florida Court subdivisions.
  • South includes the University Heights and University Place subdivisions.
  • Listing data shows about 1,690 homes with a median build year near 1949.

Two districts, one neighborhood

The City of Gainesville designates University Heights North and University Heights South as separate local historic districts. North includes the University Terrace and Florida Court subdivisions. South includes the University Heights and University Place subdivisions and sits between the university and downtown.

People living there simply say University Heights. This page covers both districts. We describe the area by those subdivisions and not by a fixed boundary line.

Owner occupants, tenants and landlords

A real estate data summary for University Heights counts about 1,690 homes, mostly compact single-family houses with a median size around 1,330 square feet. It also shows that only about a third of parcels carry a homestead exemption, which suggests many houses are rented.

Rentals change the plumbing picture. Tenants notice problems first, landlords arrange repairs and the house may see heavy use in a few bathrooms. Clear descriptions of the problem, and quick access to the house, make for faster fixes. Toilet repair and drain cleaning top the list.

Tips for landlords and tenants

  • ✓
    Tenants: report early. A small drip is cheap to fix. A hidden one can damage a floor.
  • ✓
    Tenants: no wipes or grease. Both are common causes of rental drain blockages even when labeled flushable.
  • ✓
    Landlords: label the shutoffs. A tag on the main valve helps tenants stop a leak fast.
  • ✓
    Landlords: keep a repair record. Dates and photos of repairs help when a problem returns.

Older historic homes

Houses in the North district date to the 1920s through the early 1950s, so many have been remodeled. Instead of assuming what is behind a wall, we recommend a leak check or camera inspection to show the condition of the lines. Exterior changes in a local historic district can involve city review, so ask the City of Gainesville historic preservation staff if a project might affect the outside of a house.

Around University Heights

University Heights is covered along with the rest of Gainesville. Pleasant Street is the nearest other historic residential district, and Duckpond lies on the other side of downtown.
