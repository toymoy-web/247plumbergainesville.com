What Irma taught Alachua about water

When Hurricane Irma came through in September 2017, Alachua's three city wells near downtown suffered a week of contamination and service outages. Floodwater also rushed through the Turkey Creek subdivision. For a lot of households, that was the first time they thought about where their water came from.

The city responded with a water quality and resiliency project. A new water facility and wellfield, drilled about 300 feet into the Floridan aquifer, opened in 2025 and raised daily capacity by roughly 30 percent.

What it means at the house: a boil notice or an outage usually ends with air, sediment or low pressure in your lines when service returns. Run cold taps for a few minutes first. If a faucet or the water heater keeps spitting sediment afterward, flushing the tank or cleaning aerators is the fix, and water heater repair handles a tank that won't clear.

Burst pipe or active leak in Alachua? Before we arrive

A few minutes of the right moves limits the damage while a plumber is on the way. These fit an Alachua home on city water.

1

Close the main at the meter box.

Most Alachua homes have a shutoff beside the meter by the street, in the box City of Alachua Public Services reads. A quarter turn on a ball valve stops the flow. If the house has its own valve where the line enters, close that one first.

2

Cut power to wet areas.

Switch off breakers for any room where water is reaching outlets or cords. For a leaking electric water heater, turn off its double breaker before touching the drain or supply valves.

3

Clear the floor and stop the drains.

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors. If sewage is rising from a floor drain in a storm, as Turkey Creek homes saw during Irma, stop running water and keep everyone out of that room.

4

Call the 24/7 line.

Call (954) 697-4920 and say what you shut off and what is still running. An Alachua dispatch goes out at any hour, and night rates, if they apply, are quoted first.

Your utility is the city, not GRU

Alachua residents inside city limits get water and sewer from City of Alachua Public Services, not Gainesville Regional Utilities. The meter, mains and the public sewer belong to the city. Everything on your side of the meter, including the buried service line and the sewer lateral, is the owner's.

So if your neighbors lose water too, call the city first. If it's only your house, the problem is almost always on your side, and that's where we come in. Businesses in Progress Park have the same split, plus backflow and restroom demands covered by commercial plumbing.

Storm season checklist for Alachua homes

  • ✓
    Find your cleanout. If sewer backs up during a heavy storm, knowing where the outdoor cleanout is saves time.
  • ✓
    Keep drains clear before the rain. A partly clogged lateral is the one that backs up first when the ground is saturated.
  • ✓
    Store a few gallons of water. Irma showed that even a city system can be out for days.
  • ✓
    Know your main shutoff. If a line breaks while the house is soaked, you want the water off fast.
