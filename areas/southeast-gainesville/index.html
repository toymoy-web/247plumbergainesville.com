Southeast Gainesville · Gainesville, FL

Plumber in the Southeast Gainesville Historic District

The Southeast Gainesville Historic District is a small residential district just southeast of downtown. The City of Gainesville says it began with post-Civil War development and that its homes housed downtown merchants and professionals.

A compact area of older houses next to a creek raises its own practical plumbing questions. Call (954) 697-4920 to talk through a water line, a drain or a renovation.

Southeast Gainesville at a glance

  • Local and National Register historic district next to downtown.
  • Edges: East University Avenue, SE 9th Street, SE 5th Avenue and Sweetwater Branch.
  • 94 historic buildings in the National Register listing.
  • Homes from the turn of the century onward, per the City of Gainesville.

Where the district is

The district is bounded by East University Avenue, Southeast 9th Street, Southeast 5th Avenue and Sweetwater Branch, according to the National Register listing summary. It is part of the older residential fabric on the east side of downtown.

It is small. The listing counts 94 historic buildings. We describe it by these edges and not by loose neighborhood lines.

A small district of older homes beside a creek

The City of Gainesville traces the district to post-Civil War development, with houses from around the turn of the century that served downtown merchants and professionals. Homes of that age are usually renovated in stages.

Sweetwater Branch forms one edge of the district. We don't assume that a creek nearby means any particular problem, but wet ground and tree roots are worth considering when a yard line or sewer lateral is in question. A leak check or camera inspection gives an answer based on the actual pipe.

Before you buy or renovate here

  • ✓
    Ask what has been replaced. A seller may know which lines were redone and when.
  • ✓
    Run a camera down the main drain. It shows cracks, offsets and root intrusion before they become expensive.
  • ✓
    Test water pressure. Low pressure can point to a restricted or leaking line.
  • ✓
    Check with the city on exterior changes. As a local historic district, exterior work can involve review by City of Gainesville historic preservation staff.

When a repair means replacement

Sometimes a line is too far gone for patches. A water line repair or sewer line repair replaces only the failing run. Whole house repiping is a larger step and is only worth considering when problems keep coming back.

Around the district
