An old town with old plumbing questions

Micanopy was incorporated in 1837 and calls itself the oldest continuously inhabited community in Florida's interior. The town covers just over a square mile, and its historic homes line Cholokka Boulevard near the town hall, the library and the Micanopy Historical Society Museum.

Houses that old were plumbed long after they were built, often in stages. It's common to find a mix of galvanized, copper and plastic pipe in one crawlspace, with joints between them that corrode first.

Outside the town core, the plumbing looks completely different. Newer homes on acreage run on their own well, pressure tank and septic system, with no utility to call when something stops.

Across 32667 the everyday calls are septic service and well pump repair. North in 32608, where homes are on GRU water and sewer, drain cleaning and water heater work are more common.

Burst pipe, dead pump or backup in Micanopy

Old homes, wells and septic all change the first steps. These cover the situations we hear about most from Micanopy.

1

On a well, cut the pump first.

Switch off the breaker marked for the well pump. That stops the pump from feeding the leak, and it protects the pump from running dry.

2

In town, close the meter valve.

Homes on the town water system have a shutoff at the meter. Older houses may not have a working valve inside, so the meter is the safer choice.

3

Stop using drains if it's a backup.

Sewage rising in a tub or toilet means the septic tank or its line is full. Every flush makes it worse until it's pumped or cleared.

4

Call our 24/7 line.

Why septic and water quality come up so often

In 2001 the town asked the Legislature for funding to study replacing septic tanks with central sewer, and to fix taste, odor and staining complaints with its water system. That request is two decades old, but it explains why septic tanks are still part of daily life here.

Wells in the area draw from the Floridan aquifer. One permitted well near the I-75 interchange at CR 234 is 180 feet deep. Water from wells like that is hard and sometimes carries iron, so staining in sinks and toilets is common, and a water test is the place to start.

Keeping an old Micanopy house dry

  • ✓
    Check the crawlspace twice a year. Look for drips at pipe joints and any standing water under the floor.
  • ✓
    Insulate exposed pipe. Raised homes lose heat under the floor, and the rare hard freeze splits bare pipe first.
  • ✓
    Pump the septic tank on schedule. Every three to five years for most households.
  • ✓
    Watch for rust-colored water. It points to old galvanized pipe or iron in the well, and a whole-house water softener or filter handles the iron.
