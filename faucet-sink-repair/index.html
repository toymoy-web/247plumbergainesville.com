Emergency Faucet and Sink Repair in Gainesville, FL

Faucet and sink repair covers the kitchen, bathroom, laundry and outdoor fixtures you use every day. That means dripping spouts, leaking handles, weak flow, loose faucets, leaking drains under the sink and the shutoff valves that feed them. It also covers replacing a faucet or sink outright.

Most of this is routine work. Water pouring from under a sink cabinet isn't, and the phone is answered 24/7 for that.

Emergency faucet and sink repair: burst supply lines and stuck valves

A dripping faucet can wait, but a burst supply line or a stuck shutoff valve cannot. Water spraying under a sink, a braided line that has split or a leaking valve behind a washing machine can flood a kitchen in minutes.

The angle stop under the sink is the first thing to try. If it breaks off in your hand or will not close, the main shutoff is the next step, and that is the moment to call.

In the first minutes

  1. Turn the angle stop under the sink clockwise to close it.
  2. If there is no stop or it fails, close the main shutoff.
  3. Empty the cabinet and put a bucket under the leak.
  4. Photograph the damage before cleanup.

Hard water and Gainesville faucets

Every faucet in a GRU home runs water at about 8.2 grains per gallon of hardness. The minerals leave white crust on the spout and, more importantly, inside the cartridge and aerator where you can't see them. Handles get stiff, flow gets weak, and seals wear faster against the gritty buildup.

An aerator is the easiest thing to fix yourself. Unscrew it, soak it in white vinegar for an hour, rinse and reinstall. If the flow is still weak afterward, the cartridge is the next suspect.

If scale is showing up on every fixture and the water heater too, the long-term fix is at the source. A properly sized water softener handles hardness, and under-sink water filtration handles taste and chlorine.

Older stem-style valves still turn up in the historic homes of the Duckpond and Pleasant Street, while rentals near UF, from Midtown out along SW 20th Avenue, go through cheap faucets fast. Across the whole city, GRU's hard water leaves scale inside cartridges and aerators.

The shutoff valve under the sink

Many older homes still have multi-turn shutoff valves under their sinks. After years of hard water and no use, they either won't close or start dripping the moment someone turns them. That usually gets discovered in the middle of an emergency.

When we work on a faucet, we test the shutoffs too. A stuck or leaking valve gets replaced with a quarter-turn valve that closes with a flick, so the next person who needs it can actually use it.

Repair or replace the faucet?

Good-quality faucets are built to be serviced. Replacing the cartridge on a name-brand faucet usually costs far less than a new one and brings it back to like-new operation.

Replacement makes sense when parts are no longer available, the finish is failing, the body itself is cracked, or you want a different style. A kitchen remodel is the natural time, and so is a bathroom project like a tub-to-shower conversion. We install faucets you buy yourself and check the new supply lines and valves while we're there.

  1. 1
    Leaking drains. Slip nuts, trap washers and basket strainers under kitchen and bathroom sinks are resealed or replaced.
  2. 2
    New sinks. Undermount and drop-in sinks are set, sealed and connected, with the drain reworked if the new sink sits differently.
  3. 3
    Laundry and utility sinks. Leaking faucets and drains fixed, and new tubs installed.
  4. 4
    Hose bibs and outdoor faucets. Dripping spigots rebuilt or replaced, with frost-proof models where the pipe is exposed.

An outdoor spigot that split during a freeze is often part of a bigger problem inside the wall. That's a job for emergency burst pipe repair.
