Water Line Repair & Replacement · Gainesville, FL

Emergency Water Line Repair and Replacement in Gainesville, FL

Water line repair fixes the buried service line that carries water from the meter to your house. It is a different job from repiping, which replaces the pipe inside the walls. When this one leaks, the house can look perfectly dry while the meter keeps turning and a patch of lawn stays soggy.

A broken service line can leave you with no water at all. If that's where you are, the line is answered 24/7.

Water line work at a glance

  • Covers the line from the meter to the house.
  • Spot repair, full replacement or trenchless pull.
  • Utility locates requested through Sunshine 811 before digging.
  • No water at the house? Answered 24/7.

Meter to house: whose pipe is it?

GRU owns and maintains the water main and the meter. The pipe on your side of the meter, the one running under the yard to the house, belongs to the property owner. So does the job of finding and fixing a leak in it.

That split matters when you call. If the leak is on the street side of the meter or at the meter itself, call GRU. If the meter's low-flow indicator spins with the house shutoff closed, the leak is almost always on your side, somewhere in this buried line.

If the leak turns out to be inside the house after all, the job moves to leak detection or, for a line under the foundation, professional slab leak repair.

Older service lines in the historic districts near downtown, including the Duckpond and Pleasant Street, may still be galvanized steel that rusts from the inside. In newer areas like Haile Plantation and Tioga, a break is more often a line nicked by irrigation or landscaping work, or a worn fitting at the meter.

Emergency water line repair: no water, or water where it should not be

A failed service line shows up in two ways. Either the water disappears from the house, or it appears where it should not, like a soft patch of yard, a flooded meter box or a spring where there was none. Both count as emergencies.

Water that is surfacing is being lost by the minute and can wash out soil under pavement. A house with no water has no toilets, no hot water and nothing to cook with. Water on the street side of the meter is GRU's to fix, and GRU lists (352) 334-2711 as its 24-hour water and wastewater line.

Before you call

  1. Look at the meter with every tap off. A moving dial means water is escaping on your side.
  2. Mark where water is surfacing and take a photo.
  3. Ask a neighbor whether their water is also off, which separates a street problem from yours.
  4. Do not dig near the line yourself, since other utilities may be close.

Signs the service line is failing

  • !
    A soggy strip of lawn that doesn't dry out. Especially in a straight line between the meter and the house.
  • !
    The meter dial moves with the house valve shut. The water is leaving between the two.
  • !
    Low pressure at every fixture at once. A line that is leaking or narrowed feeds the whole house poorly.
  • !
    A dip or small sinkhole over the line's path. Escaping water carries sand away underground, and it matters more on Gainesville's limestone.
  • !
    Brown water throughout the house after work in the street. Old galvanized service lines shed rust when pressure changes.

Digging in Gainesville ground

On the west side of the city, limestone can sit just a few feet under the sand. Trenching through it is slower than digging in sand alone, and it's one reason we look at trenchless options early on those properties.

Big oaks are the other obstacle. A line that runs under a mature tree's root zone is hard to reach, and cutting major roots can harm the tree. A new line bored along a different route often makes more sense than following the old one.

Florida law requires a locate request through Sunshine 811 before any digging, so gas, electric and communication lines are marked first. We place that request as part of the job.

Repair options, from smallest to largest

  1. 1
    Spot repair. One leak in otherwise good pipe gets dug out and fixed at that point.
  2. 2
    Full replacement by trench. A new line runs from the meter to the house along an open trench, and the old line is abandoned.
  3. 3
    Trenchless replacement. A new line is pulled or bored underground between two small pits, useful under driveways, walks, landscaping and trees.

A single leak in a copper or plastic line is usually a spot repair. Galvanized or polybutylene lines are usually worth replacing outright, since the next leak is already on its way. If the pipes inside the house are the same age, pair this with repiping work.

Service lines outside the city

On larger lots in Newberry and other outlying towns, service lines can run a long way from the road, and the meter may sit well out of sight of the house. A long line leaking slowly can go unnoticed for months. Buried pool lines fail the same way, and professional pool leak detection tests them.

Homes on private wells don't have a utility service line at all. Their buried line runs from the well and pressure tank to the house, and private well pump repair covers that system.

Dig-safe locate requirements under Florida's Underground Facility Damage Prevention and Safety Act (Sunshine 811). Pipe work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Replacing the line from the meter to the house

A common request is a quote to replace the entire line between the meter and the house. We walk the route, locate the existing pipe and quote the work as a repair of one section or a full replacement.

Houses on wells have a similar line between the well and the house, and it can leak where nobody looks. If you think old pipe may be lead or something else unusual, tell us on the call so we identify the material before quoting.

Low pressure, wet meter box or water from the ground

Low pressure that started suddenly points to a leak, a stuck valve or a failing regulator. Water bubbling from the ground or filling the meter box points to the line itself.

On the street side of the meter the pipe is GRU's responsibility, and from the meter to the house it belongs to the owner. We tell you which side the problem is on before any digging starts.
