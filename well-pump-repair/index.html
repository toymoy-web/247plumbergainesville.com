Emergency well pump repair: no water at the house

With a well, no water means the pump, the pressure tank, the switch or the power. A house on a well that has gone dry has no toilets, no showers and no drinking water, which makes it an emergency for any household with children, older adults or animals.

Wells outside the city have no utility to call. The whole system belongs to the homeowner, so the fastest route back to running water is a visit that tests each part in order.

Quick checks before you call

  1. Check the breaker and the switch at the well.
  2. Read the pressure gauge at the tank. A reading near zero points to the pump or its switch.
  3. Listen for the pump switching on and off every few seconds, which usually means a failing pressure tank.
  4. Do not run the pump dry while you troubleshoot.

Wells in rural Alachua County

Homes outside GRU's water service area, on larger lots west, north and east of Gainesville, usually draw water from a private well into the Floridan aquifer. The same limestone that supplies the city supplies these wells, just without a treatment plant in between.

Many homes use a submersible pump down in the well, feeding a pressure tank in a pump house or garage. The tank holds a cushion of air that keeps pressure steady and stops the pump from starting every time someone opens a tap. When that air cushion is lost, the pump short-cycles and burns itself out early.

Florida's summer storms are hard on wells too. Lightning strikes and power surges regularly take out pump controls and pressure switches, and sometimes the pump motor itself.

Wells are rare inside the city, where GRU supplies water, but common in the rural areas around it: acreage off Millhopper Road in the northwest, the countryside toward Monteocha in the northeast, and lots west toward Jonesville and Archer. Micanopy's wide 32667 ZIP to the south is mostly well country too.

No water? Check these before you call

  1. 1
    Check the breaker. Well pumps have their own breaker, often a double-pole one. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop.
  2. 2
    Look at the pressure gauge. Near the tank. If it reads zero or near zero, the pump isn't building pressure.
  3. 3
    Listen at the tank. A clicking pressure switch with no pump running points to controls or the pump.
  4. 4
    Check any whole-house filter. A clogged sediment filter can choke flow even with a good pump.

If none of those restore water, call. Pumps and controls work on high voltage, and diagnosing them safely takes the right meters and experience.

Well permits and water testing

New wells and major well work in Florida are permitted through the water management districts. Alachua County sits across two of them, the Suwannee River and St. Johns River districts, depending on location. Pump and tank replacements on an existing well are usually plumbing-side work and don't need a new well permit.

Well owners are responsible for their own water quality. Testing for bacteria after flooding or any well repair is a good habit, and hardness, iron and sulfur are worth testing for before choosing treatment. Water filtration and a whole-house water softener handle what the test finds.
