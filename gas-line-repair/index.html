Emergency Gas Line Installation and Repair in Gainesville, FL

Gas line work covers the pipe that carries natural gas or propane from the meter or tank to your appliances. That includes repairing leaks, running new lines for a range, dryer, water heater or generator, and replacing old or undersized piping.

If you smell gas right now, leave the house and call 911 and your gas provider from outside first. Once the supply is safe, our line is answered 24/7 for the repair.

If you smell gas, do this in order

  1. 1
    Get everyone out. Leave doors open behind you if it's quick to do so. Don't stop to gather belongings.
  2. 2
    Don't create a spark. No light switches, no appliances, no phone calls until you're outside and away from the house.
  3. 3
    Call 911 and your gas provider from outside. GRU for natural gas inside its service area, or your propane company for a tank.
  4. 4
    Wait for the all-clear. The utility or fire department will shut off and check the supply before anyone goes back in.
  5. 5
    Then schedule the repair. Once the gas is off and the area is safe, a plumber can find and fix the leaking pipe or fitting.

Natural gas and propane both carry an added odorant that smells like rotten eggs or sulfur. Take that smell seriously even if it's faint.

Emergency gas line repair: what counts as an emergency

The smell of gas is an emergency. So is a hissing sound near a pipe, a pilot that will not stay lit with a gas smell nearby, or a suspected leak after a storm or digging. Leave the building before you do anything else.

Call from outside. Do not flip switches, light anything or use a phone indoors. GRU lists (352) 334-2550 as its 24-hour natural gas emergency number, and propane customers call their supplier. Call us for the line repair once the area is safe.

Treat these as emergencies

  • A rotten-egg smell indoors or near a meter.
  • A hissing or whistling sound at a pipe or appliance.
  • Dead grass or bubbling water over a buried gas line.
  • Any gas smell after a storm, a flood or digging near the line.

Natural gas and propane in the Gainesville area

GRU supplies natural gas across much of Gainesville, alongside its electric and water service. Homes inside that service area can run gas ranges, dryers, water heaters and furnaces from a GRU meter.

Outside the gas service area, many rural homes use propane from a tank on the property. The piping inside the house works much the same, but appliances need to be set up for the right fuel, and the regulator and tank connection belong to the propane supplier.

Switching an electric appliance to gas is a common reason for a new line. GRU offers rebates on some conversions, with conditions, including for a natural gas water heater. Check the current terms with GRU before you commit.

GRU supplies natural gas to much of the city, from established neighborhoods near downtown to newer homes in Haile Plantation, where many listings show gas connected. Past the gas mains, in rural parts of the county toward Monteocha or Archer, homes run on propane tanks instead, and those lines are checked the same way.

Why gas line size matters

Every gas appliance needs a certain volume of gas to run properly. When a new appliance is added to an old line, the line can come up short when several appliances run together. A tankless water heater is the most common culprit. It demands a lot of gas at once, and on an undersized line it throws ignition errors that look like a faulty heater.

That's why we size lines by adding up the appliances on them and the length of the run, using the standard sizing tables in the fuel gas code. If a tankless unit keeps faulting, tankless water heater service and a gas supply check often go together.

How a gas line job is done safely

  1. 1
    Shut off and plan. The gas is shut off at the meter or tank, and the route and pipe size are planned.
  2. 2
    Run the pipe. Black iron, coated flexible gas tubing or other approved material, depending on the location.
  3. 3
    Pressure test. The new or repaired line is tested under pressure and checked for any drop before gas is turned back on.
  4. 4
    Permit and inspection. New gas piping is permitted work in Gainesville and Alachua County.
  5. 5
    Restore and relight. Gas is restored, appliances are relit and every connection is checked for leaks.
