Buying an Older Gainesville Home? A Plumbing Checklist

By the Gainesville 24/7 Plumbing team · Published September 24, 2026.

The short answer

In an older Gainesville home, the plumbing questions that matter most are what the supply pipe is made of, what the sewer lateral looks like inside, how old the water heater is, and whether past work was permitted. A standard home inspection usually covers the visible fixtures, not the buried sewer line.

For a house built before the 1980s, adding a sewer camera inspection before closing is one of the cheapest ways to avoid an expensive surprise.

Why older Gainesville homes need a closer look

Gainesville's most sought-after older neighborhoods are also its oldest plumbing. The Duckpond, officially the Northeast Gainesville Residential District, holds 229 historic buildings on about 1,660 acres. Pleasant Street, the Southeast district and both University Heights districts hold many homes from the same era.

Houses from before about 1950 were commonly plumbed with galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron or clay drains. Some mid-century homes used fiber pipe for the sewer lateral. All of these can work for decades and then fail in ways you can't see from inside the house.

None of this is a reason to walk away from a house you love. It's a reason to know what you're buying and budget for it.

The checklist

  • ✓
    Identify the supply pipe. Look where the main enters, at the water heater and under sinks. Galvanized is dull gray metal with threaded joints. Polybutylene is gray plastic, often stamped PB2110.
  • ✓
    Run every faucet and shower at once. A big pressure drop, or brown water that takes a while to clear, points to old galvanized.
  • ✓
    Order a sewer camera inspection. It shows roots, cracks, sags and collapsed sections in the lateral, with video you keep.
  • ✓
    Read the water heater label. The serial number encodes the build date. Past about ten years, budget for a replacement.
  • ✓
    Look under every sink and around toilets. Water stains, soft floors and old repairs tell you what's been happening.
  • ✓
    Check the shutoff valves. Old valves that won't turn or that drip when moved are a small cost now and a big problem later.
  • ✓
    Ask for permit history. Repipes, water heaters and sewer work in Gainesville are permitted. A record means someone inspected it.
  • ✓
    Ask the seller about past leaks and backups. Florida sellers are generally expected to disclose known material defects, and the answer tells you where to look.

Budgeting for an older home's plumbing

It helps to think of an older house's plumbing in three layers, each with its own timeline. The supply pipe carries water in. The drain and sewer lines carry it out. The water heater and fixtures sit on top of both.

Supply pipe that's galvanized or polybutylene tends to be the first big project, because pressure drops and leaks start without much warning. A sewer lateral in clay or cast iron may run fine for years with occasional cleaning, until the camera shows a break. A water heater is the most predictable of the three, since its age is printed on the label.

Knowing which layer is closest to the end of its life lets you plan the order of work instead of reacting to whichever fails first.

What a reassuring inspection looks like

Good news in an older home looks like copper or PEX supply lines from a past repipe. It looks like a camera run with clean joints or a newer PVC lateral, a water heater under about eight years old, and permits on file for past work. Strong pressure with several fixtures open and clear water from the first draw are good signs too.

Historic district rules and plumbing

Homes in Gainesville's local historic districts come with design review for exterior changes, handled through the city's historic preservation process. Interior plumbing work generally isn't what that review covers, but work that changes the exterior, like a new vent through a visible roof slope or an exterior equipment enclosure, may be. Check with the city before planning anything visible.

Plumbing permits still apply to repipes, water heaters and sewer work, the same as anywhere in the city.

Using what you find in negotiations

A sewer camera video showing a collapsed lateral is concrete. So is a pipe that's clearly polybutylene. Those findings give you something specific to take to the seller: a repair before closing, a credit, or a price adjustment.

Get a written quote for any major item while you're still in the inspection period. A walkthrough for a repipe or a sewer replacement quote is usually free, and it turns a vague worry into a number.

Also ask your insurance agent early. Some Florida insurers ask about pipe type, and knowing whether they'll write a policy on the house as it stands can change your plans.

Getting the right inspections done

Start with a sewer camera inspection, which covers the part of the house a standard inspection skips. If the supply pipe is galvanized or polybutylene, a repiping walkthrough gives you a real number for negotiations.

An old water heater can be priced through new water heater installation at the same time. The same checks apply to older homes in the historic downtown of High Springs.
