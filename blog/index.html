What this blog covers

Most plumbing advice online is written for everywhere, which means it fits nowhere in particular. These guides are written for Gainesville: water from the Floridan aquifer at about 8.2 grains of hardness, sand over shallow limestone on the west side, clay to the east, pre-war pipe in the historic districts, and wells and septic tanks across the rural county.

You'll find guides on reading a high GRU bill, spotting a slab leak, choosing a water heater, preparing pipes for a hard freeze, buying an older home, and living with a well or a septic system. Each one ends with what you can do yourself and when it's time to call.

How these guides are written

Every local fact in these guides comes from a public source, such as GRU's water quality reports, the city and county utilities, state agencies or published building codes, and the source is named in the post.

We don't invent statistics, customer stories or quotes. Where a number varies from house to house, like the cost of a repair, the guide explains what moves it rather than guessing a figure. Each guide also separates what a homeowner can safely do from the jobs that need a licensed plumber.

Guides are grouped by topic: water bills and leaks, slabs and older homes, water heaters and hard water, drains and sewer lines, and wells and septic systems for homes outside the city. Pick the one closest to what you're dealing with.
