Sinkhole or Slab Leak in Gainesville, FL? How to Tell Them Apart

By the Gainesville 24/7 Plumbing team · Published September 24, 2026 · Updated October 12, 2026.

The short answer

Most new cracks in a Gainesville home are ordinary settling or the result of a water leak, not a sinkhole. A leaking line under or near a slab can wash sand into gaps in the limestone below, and the symptoms can look similar. So the first step is always to rule out a leak with a simple meter test.

If the meter is still and cracks keep widening, doors start sticking, or a depression appears in the yard, that's when to bring in a geotechnical engineer and call your insurer.

Why this question comes up so often here

Alachua County sits on karst, limestone that slowly dissolves as slightly acidic groundwater moves through it. The Cody Scarp, an old shoreline, runs through the Gainesville area. Southwest of it, the Ocala Limestone lies just a few feet under the sand. Northeast of it, a thicker blanket of clay covers the rock.

Devil's Millhopper, a sinkhole about 120 feet deep, sits inside the city. In October 2020, a sinkhole near NW 13th Place widened past 100 feet and spread across four properties over two months, with a county geologist measuring it most days.

Events like that make headlines, which is why any new crack can feel alarming. In practice, water is a far more common reason for ground movement under a house, and water is the part you can fix.

Side by side: slab leak signs vs. sinkhole warning signs

Some signs overlap. The ones in the middle column point most strongly to plumbing, and the ones in the right column point to the ground.

Slab leak and sinkhole warning signs compared.

What to do first, in order

  1. 1
    Run the meter test. Turn off every fixture and watch the meter's low-flow dial for ten minutes. Movement means water is escaping.
  2. 2
    Check for a warm spot or a running sound. Both point strongly to a supply leak under the slab.
  3. 3
    Mark the cracks. Draw a pencil line at each end of every crack and write the date beside it. Growth over weeks is the key question.
  4. 4
    Photograph everything. Inside and outside, with dates, before anything is repaired.
  5. 5
    Fix any leak you find. It stops the washout. Then keep watching the marked cracks.
  6. 6
    Call a geotechnical engineer if cracks keep growing. Especially if doors stick, the yard sinks or nothing leaked.

What to do if a hole opens in the yard

Keep people and pets well away from it, since the edges can keep collapsing. Don't fill it yourself with dirt or debris. If it's close to the house, a road or a utility line, or the house shows sudden new damage, call 911 and leave the structure until it's checked.

Then call your insurer. In Alachua County, the Environmental Protection Department has geologists who track sinkhole activity, and your insurer will arrange its own professional testing if you file a claim.

How Florida insurance treats each

The difference matters a lot for your claim. A slab leak is a plumbing event. Many Florida homeowners policies cover access to a sudden leak and the water damage it caused, but not replacing the pipe itself.

Sinkholes fall under a separate section of Florida law. Insurers must cover catastrophic ground cover collapse, a narrow category that generally requires an abrupt collapse, a visible depression, structural damage and the home being condemned and ordered vacated. Broader sinkhole coverage is usually an optional add-on. Sinkhole claims also involve testing by qualified professionals, such as engineers or geologists.

Because of that, a documented leak with a repair invoice is a very different claim from a suspected sinkhole. Ruling out the leak first keeps your claim clear and avoids paying for ground testing you didn't need.

Lowering the risk around your own house

  • ✓
    Fix leaks promptly. Leaking supply and sewer lines are a common local cause of soil washout under slabs.
  • ✓
    Send roof water away from the foundation. Downspouts that dump next to the slab concentrate water right where you don't want it.
  • ✓
    Watch irrigation. A zone that runs too long or leaks near the house saturates the same soil.
  • ✓
    Don't ignore a sinking spot in the lawn. Small depressions are worth a look before they grow.

Clay ground is a different story

East of the city, toward Hawthorne, thicker clay covers the limestone. Large sinkholes are less common there, but clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, and that movement cracks slabs and stresses the pipes inside them. The advice is the same: rule out a leak first, then watch whether the cracks keep growing.

Gainesville local note: reporting and insurance

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection says it does not inspect possible sinkholes and that if there is a risk of physical harm you should call local law enforcement. The Florida Geological Survey takes subsidence reports through an incident report form by mail, email or fax, or by phone.

Its sinkhole FAQ advises you to mark and secure a hole, keep children and pets away, avoid putting water in it and contact your insurance company right away if a sinkhole threatens your home. Florida Statutes Section 627.706 defines sinkhole loss as structural damage to the covered building, including the foundation, caused by sinkhole activity.

A plumber can rule a leak in or out with a pressure test. We do that in Micanopy, High Springs and Gainesville, and our slab leak page explains how.

Sinkhole and slab leak questions

A leak can wash sand into existing gaps in the limestone and cause a local depression or settling. That's different from a geological sinkhole, but it's a good reason to fix leaks quickly on karst ground.

Alachua County has real, documented sinkhole activity, especially on the west side where limestone is close to the surface. Most houses never have one, and most cracks have other causes.

If there's any sign of a leak, start with the plumbing, since it's quicker and cheaper to rule out. If there's no leak and cracks keep growing, call a geotechnical or structural engineer.

Policies must cover catastrophic ground cover collapse, which is narrowly defined. Broader sinkhole coverage is usually optional. Check your declarations page.

It varies. Marking crack ends with a date lets you see whether they're growing over weeks, which is the most useful information you can give an engineer.

Florida DEP says to call local law enforcement if anyone is at risk, to mark and secure the hole and to contact your insurer. Sinkhole reports go to the Florida Geological Survey through its incident report form. A plumber can test the pipes to rule a leak in or out.
