Water Heater Installation and Replacement in Gainesville, FL

Water heater installation means removing the old unit and setting a new one, connected, vented, permitted and tested. Replacement is the common version of it: a tank reaches the end of its life and a new one goes in the same spot, or a different type goes in if the household wants a change.

If your tank has already failed and is leaking, the phone is answered 24/7. Most replacements can be done the same day or the next.

Choosing a new water heater in Gainesville

The four realistic options each fit a different house. What matters most: whether you have natural gas at the street, how much space the unit has, and how long you plan to stay.

Water heater types compared for Gainesville homes.
TypeGood fit whenWatch out for
Electric tank.No gas service, simple like-for-like swap.Highest running cost of the four on GRU electric rates.
Natural gas tank.The home already has GRU gas or it's available at the street.Needs proper venting and a gas line sized for it.
Heat pump (hybrid) tank.Garage or large utility room with space and air around the unit.Taller unit, a little noise, cools the space it sits in.
Tankless (gas).High hot-water demand, tight space, long-term owners.Needs yearly descaling on Gainesville's hard water and a larger gas line.

A heat pump unit pulls heat from the surrounding air, so a Florida garage is close to ideal for one. It also dehumidifies the space a little as it runs.

Switching to natural gas? Check GRU's rebate first

GRU offers rebates to customers who convert an electric, oil or propane water heater to natural gas. The program does not cover replacing an existing gas heater with a new one, and the home has to be inside GRU's natural gas service area.

The rebate also requires installation by a GRU Natural Gas Partnering Contractor. Check your eligibility and the current contractor list with GRU before you schedule anything, so a conversion doesn't lose the rebate on a technicality. A new gas heater may also need a new or larger supply, which is handled through gas line installation.

Replacements tend to come in waves by neighborhood. Subdivisions built in the 1990s and 2000s, including much of Haile Plantation and the Tower Road corridor, are now on their second or third water heater, while rental houses near UF often need a replacement between tenants.

Getting the size right

Tank size is less important than first-hour rating, the amount of hot water a unit can deliver in a busy hour. A 40-gallon tank is usually enough for one or two people. Families of four or more, or homes with a large soaking tub, generally do better with 50 gallons or a higher-output unit.

Undersizing is a common mistake on replacements. The old tank was often picked years ago for a smaller household. Tell us who lives there now and how the hot water gets used.

What an installation includes

  1. 1
    Permit. Replacing a water heater is permitted work. Inside Gainesville city limits that goes through the city, and unincorporated addresses go through Alachua County.
  2. 2
    Drain and remove the old unit. The old heater is disconnected and taken out of the install space.
  3. 3
    Set and connect the new unit. New connectors and shutoff, and a drain pan where it sits over finished space.
  4. 4
    Pressure and safety items. An expansion tank if the system needs one, and a relief valve discharge line piped to an approved spot.
  5. 5
    Gas or electric hookup and venting. Gas units get a combustion and venting check. Electric units get a check of breaker size and wiring.
  6. 6
    Fill, fire and test. We fill the tank before power goes on, set the temperature and check every connection.

Signs it's time to replace rather than repair

  • ✓
    Water seeping from the tank body. Once the inner tank rusts through, no part fixes it.
  • ✓
    The tank is past 10 years old and needs a major part. A gas valve or a second element on an old tank rarely pays off.
  • ✓
    Rusty hot water even after an anode change. The tank lining is breaking down.
  • ✓
    Loud rumbling that flushing no longer quiets. Hardened sediment has built up past what a flush can clear.

If the heater is younger and the problem is a single part, emergency water heater repair is usually the better value.

What changes the price of a new water heater

  • $
    Type and size. Electric tanks cost least up front, and tankless and heat pump units cost most.
  • $
    Conversions. Switching electric to gas, or tank to tankless, adds gas line, venting or electrical work.
  • $
    Location. An attic or tight closet swap takes longer than an open garage.
  • $
    Code updates. Older installs often need an expansion tank, pan or discharge line brought up to current code.
  • $
    Permit fees. Set by the city or county and shown on the quote.
