Humming, dead or leaking? Start here

Always switch the disposal off at the wall switch, and unplug it or flip its breaker, before trying any of these. Never put a hand inside the unit.

  • ✓
    It hums but doesn't spin. Something is jammed. Most units have a hex socket on the bottom center, and turning it back and forth with the matching wrench frees the plates.
  • ✓
    It's completely dead. Press the red reset button on the bottom. If it trips again right away, the motor is struggling or failing.
  • ✓
    It spins but drains slowly. The clog is usually in the drain line past the disposal, not the disposal itself.
  • ✓
    Water drips from the bottom. The internal seal has failed, and that means a new disposal.

If the reset and the hex wrench don't do it, stop there and call. Forcing a jammed unit burns out the motor.

What not to put down a disposal

Grease tops the list. It goes down liquid, then hardens further along the line where the disposal can't reach it. Pour cooking grease into a can, let it set and throw it out. In GRU homes, grease also ends up in the public sewer, which is why the utility runs a grease management program for businesses.

Stringy and starchy food causes most jams: celery, corn husks, onion skins, potato peels, and large amounts of rice or pasta that swell into paste. Fruit pits and bones can jam the plates. Small amounts of soft scraps with plenty of cold running water are what a disposal is built for.

If the kitchen line keeps backing up even when the disposal is used carefully, the pipe itself may have years of buildup. Drain cleaning clears it, and heavy grease may need hydro jetting.

Disposal calls cluster in the apartments and rental houses near UF, from Midtown out along SW 20th Avenue and Archer Road, where a new tenant every year means new habits. In family neighborhoods like Haile Plantation and the Millhopper area, the usual culprits are holiday grease and fibrous peels.

Disposals on septic systems

Homes on septic tanks, common across rural Alachua County, can use a disposal, but it adds solids to the tank. That means the tank fills faster and needs pumping more often.

If your home is on septic, use the disposal lightly, keep grease out entirely, and plan on a shorter pumping interval. Our professional septic service can help set a schedule that fits how you use it.

What a disposal installation includes

  1. 1
    Remove the old unit. Disconnect power, drain and dishwasher lines, and take the old disposal off its mount.
  2. 2
    New mount and flange. A fresh seal at the sink so the top doesn't leak.
  3. 3
    Dishwasher connection. On a new disposal, the dishwasher inlet has a knockout plug that must be removed. Skipping it is a common reason a dishwasher suddenly won't drain.
  4. 4
    Drain alignment. The discharge tube and trap are lined up and sealed.
  5. 5
    Test. Run it with water, run the dishwasher drain, and check every joint for drips.

Repair or replace?

Jams, flange leaks and loose connections are repairs. A leak from the bottom, a cracked housing, or a motor that keeps tripping the reset button means the unit has reached its end. Disposals aren't built to be rebuilt internally, so replacement is the normal fix.

Size the new one to how you cook. A small household can do fine with a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower unit. Busy kitchens do better with 3/4 horsepower or more, which grinds faster and jams less.
