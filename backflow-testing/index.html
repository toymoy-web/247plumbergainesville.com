Backflow Testing and Prevention in Gainesville, FL

Backflow testing checks that a backflow preventer, the valve assembly that stops water from flowing backward into the public supply, still works. Prevention covers installing, repairing and replacing those assemblies on irrigation systems, homes and commercial buildings.

In Gainesville, GRU requires testing on a schedule and a certified tester has to file the results. A cracked assembly spraying water after a freeze is a different matter, and the line is answered 24/7 for that.

  • !

What a backflow preventer does

Normally, water flows one way, from the GRU main into your property. Backflow is when it reverses. That can happen when pressure in the main drops, during a water main break or heavy hydrant use, or when pressure on the property side climbs higher than the supply.

If that reversed water has touched fertilizer in an irrigation line, a boiler loop or a chemical process, it can contaminate the drinking water supply. A backflow preventer is a set of check valves and relief valves that stops the reversal.

GRU's cross-connection control rules require lawn irrigation connected to city water to be protected by a pressure vacuum breaker or a reduced pressure assembly. Where chemicals are injected into the irrigation system, a reduced pressure assembly is required.

Irrigation systems are where most residential backflow assemblies sit, and they're common in planned neighborhoods like Haile Plantation and Tioga and in the subdivisions along the Tower Road corridor. Medical offices and restaurants around Butler Plaza and Celebration Pointe carry their own assemblies too. Wherever yours is, the test is done right at the device.

How a backflow test works

  1. 1
    Shut down briefly. Water to the assembly is shut off for the test, usually less than half an hour.
  2. 2
    Connect the test kit. A differential pressure gauge is attached to the assembly's test cocks.
  3. 3
    Check each valve. Every check valve and relief valve is tested against its required pressure.
  4. 4
    Record and file. Results go on the test report, and the certified tester files it with GRU.
  5. 5
    Repair if needed. Worn rubber parts are replaced from a repair kit, and the assembly is retested.

Freezes and backflow assemblies

Residential backflow preventers usually sit above ground near the irrigation line, fully exposed. On the rare nights Gainesville drops well below freezing, they're often the first thing on the property to crack.

An insulated cover or a wrap before a hard freeze prevents most of the damage. If yours has already split and is spraying, shut off the irrigation supply valve and call. The repair is covered on our emergency burst pipe repair calls too.

Commercial properties and irrigation-heavy sites

GRU requires non-residential backflow assemblies to be maintained and tested every year, and it can require testing more often in some cases. Restaurants, medical offices and multi-unit buildings can carry several assemblies on one property. Our light commercial plumbing service keeps them on schedule.

Larger irrigation systems in newer subdivisions, around Alachua and west Gainesville, rely on these assemblies too. If an assembly keeps failing because of high pressure or debris, the cause may be upstream in the service line.

GRU Manual of Cross Connection Control (testing schedules and irrigation requirements), GRU backflow prevention guidance (30-day filing window), Florida Administrative Code 62-555.360, and the AWWA Manual of Practice M14 standard GRU references for cross-connection control.

Test reports, repairs and retests

Most backflow calls start with a notice and a deadline. We test the assembly, give you the test report in the form the utility asks for and repair or replace parts that fail.

If an assembly fails, ask for the repair and the retest in one visit. That keeps you on the utility's schedule.
