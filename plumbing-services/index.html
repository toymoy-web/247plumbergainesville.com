Safety checklist: call now, or can it wait until morning?

Not every plumbing problem needs a night call. This is the rough line we use when someone calls after hours, and we'll tell you honestly which side of it you're on.

Plumbing problems by urgency.
Call right awayUsually fine until morning
Water you can't shut off, or pouring from a pipe or ceiling.A dripping faucet or a running toilet.
Sewage coming up in a tub, shower or floor drain.One slow drain in one fixture.
No water to the whole house.Low pressure at a single faucet.
A leaking water heater tank.A water heater that's lukewarm.
Gas smell (leave first, call 911 and the gas provider).A higher-than-usual water bill.

If you're on the fence, call and describe it. Shutting off the main valve buys time for almost any water problem, and we'll help you find it.

Local conditions behind these services

Some of these pages exist because Gainesville is Gainesville. Hard water from the Floridan aquifer drives a lot of water heater, softener and faucet work. Sand over shallow limestone, and clay on the east side, drive slab leaks and sewer line damage. Older homes in the historic districts drive repiping and sewer camera work, and the rural county drives well and septic calls.

Each service page explains how its problem shows up here, with local sources, so you can make a decision before anyone arrives.
