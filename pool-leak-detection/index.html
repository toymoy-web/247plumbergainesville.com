Pool Leak Detection and Repair in Gainesville, FL

Pool leak detection finds where a swimming pool or spa is losing water: the shell, the fittings, or the buried plumbing that carries water to and from the equipment. Repair fixes the leaking line or fitting once it's found.

A pool leak is rarely an emergency. The water bill it causes can be, and the line is answered 24/7 if water is pooling around the equipment pad.

Pool leak service at a glance

  • Bucket test guidance before anyone comes out.
  • Pressure testing of suction and return lines.
  • Skimmers, returns, light niches and main drains checked.
  • Buried pool plumbing repaired.

Is it a leak or just evaporation? Try the bucket test

Florida sun and wind take a noticeable amount of water out of a pool every day in summer. The bucket test compares your pool to a bucket sitting in the same conditions.

  1. 1
    Fill a bucket with pool water. Set it on the first or second pool step so it sits at the same temperature.
  2. 2
    Mark both water levels. One line inside the bucket, one on the pool wall or skimmer.
  3. 3
    Run the pump as usual for 24 hours. Don't add water or use the pool.
  4. 4
    Compare the drops. If the pool dropped noticeably more than the bucket, there's a leak.
  5. 5
    Repeat with the pump off. If the loss is worse with the pump running, the leak is likely in the pressure side of the plumbing.

Why pool leaks cost more on GRU water

Refilling a leaking pool adds up quickly on a GRU bill. Wastewater charges are calculated from metered water use, so topping off a pool can raise both sides of the bill unless you have a separate irrigation meter.

GRU does consider courtesy leak adjustments when a leak is verified and the repair is documented. If a pool leak drove up your bill, keep the leak detection findings and the repair invoice together, the same way you would for a house leak.

Pools on Gainesville ground

Pool plumbing is buried in the same sand-over-limestone ground as house plumbing. Settling soil can stress the rigid PVC lines between the pool and the equipment pad, especially at fittings and where lines turn.

Tree roots are another cause. A large oak near the pool deck can reach return lines, and roots that find a weak joint make it worse. If a line needs replacing rather than patching, the work is similar to water line repair: dig at the leak, or reroute.

How a pool leak is found and fixed

  1. 1
    Bucket test results. Share what you measured. It tells us whether to focus on plumbing or the shell.
  2. 2
    Equipment check. Pump, filter, heater and valves are checked for leaks first, since they're the easiest to fix.
  3. 3
    Pressure test the lines. Each suction and return line is plugged and pressurized to see which one loses pressure.
  4. 4
    Dye test fittings. Colored dye near skimmers, returns and lights shows where water is being drawn out.
  5. 5
    Repair. A leaking line is exposed and repaired, or a fitting is resealed.
