Tankless Water Heater Service in Gainesville, FL

Tankless water heater service keeps an on-demand heater running cleanly. That means descaling the heat exchanger, cleaning the inlet filter, checking the burner and venting, and fixing faults behind error codes. It also covers new tankless installs and tank-to-tankless conversions.

In a hard-water city like Gainesville, the descaling part is not optional. Scale is the reason most tankless units here start acting up. If yours quit entirely, the line is answered 24/7.

Error code, lukewarm bursts or a unit that shuts off mid-shower?

Owners rarely call asking for a descale. They call because the unit started doing one of these things, and most of them trace back to scale or airflow.

  • !
    Hot, then cold, then hot again. Scale restricts flow through the heat exchanger, and the unit cycles on and off.
  • !
    An error code on the display. Many codes point to overheating, ignition failure or low flow, all of which scale can cause.
  • !
    Hot water takes longer than it used to. A clogged inlet filter or a narrowed exchanger slows the flow.
  • !
    The unit shuts off with two fixtures running. It can no longer heat the volume it was sized for.
  • !
    Whistling or rattling from the cabinet. Scale deposits or a fan with debris in it.

Why tankless units need descaling here

A tankless heater sends water through a narrow heat exchanger and heats it in seconds. That fast heating is exactly where minerals drop out of the water. With Gainesville's water at roughly 8.2 grains per gallon, a heat exchanger picks up a steady layer of scale.

Scale acts like insulation. The burner works harder to reach temperature, the exchanger overheats, and the unit's safety sensors shut it down. Left long enough, scale can crack the exchanger, which is the most expensive part in the unit.

Most manufacturers call for descaling on a regular schedule and more often on hard water. Once a year is a sensible starting point for a Gainesville home, sooner if the household uses a lot of hot water. A whole-house water softener upstream stretches that interval considerably.

What a descaling service involves

  1. 1
    Isolate the unit. Water and power or gas are shut off, and the service valves are opened to a pump loop.
  2. 2
    Circulate descaling solution. A food-safe descaler is pumped through the exchanger for about an hour to dissolve mineral buildup.
  3. 3
    Flush and clean the inlet screen. Fresh water rinses out the solution, and the small filter at the cold inlet is cleaned.
  4. 4
    Check combustion and venting. On gas units we inspect the burner, flame sensor, venting and condensate line.
  5. 5
    Restart and test. Flow and temperature are checked with more than one fixture open.

If your unit has no service valves, we can add them during the first visit. They make every future descale faster and cheaper.

Before anyone tells you to replace it

A common complaint from tankless owners goes like this: someone came out, said they don't service that brand, and offered a new unit instead. A tankless heater that is under 15 years old and has been descaled is usually worth diagnosing properly.

We look at the error history, the exchanger, the sensors and the gas supply before talking about replacement. Sometimes the answer is a flame rod or a fan. Sometimes the exchanger really is done, and then replacing the water heater is the honest call.

Gas supply and venting

Gas tankless units draw a lot of gas all at once. When a unit was installed on an existing tank heater's supply line, it can starve when the stove and dryer run at the same time. That shows up as ignition errors that no descale will fix. Gas line work sizes the supply to the unit.
