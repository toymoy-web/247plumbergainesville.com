Sewer Camera Inspection in Gainesville, FL

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through your drain line and records what it sees. Roots, cracks, sags, offset joints and collapsed sections show up on screen, measured by distance from the access point.

It replaces guessing with video. You see the same footage we do, and any repair recommendation is based on it. If you're dealing with a backup tonight, the line is answered 24/7.

Camera inspection at a glance

  • Video of the full lateral, yours to keep.
  • Problem spots located and marked from the surface.
  • Pre-purchase inspections for older homes.
  • No digging to find out what's wrong.

When a camera inspection is worth it

  • ✓
    The same drain has clogged two or three times. Something in the line keeps catching waste, and the camera shows what.
  • ✓
    You're buying a house built before the 1980s. Most standard home inspections don't cover the sewer line.
  • ✓
    Before hydro jetting an older line. Fragile clay, cast iron or fiber pipe needs checking before high pressure goes in.
  • ✓
    Before building over the line. A patio, pool deck or addition on top of a failing lateral turns a small repair into a big one.
  • ✓
    After a large tree comes out or a storm uproots one. Roots that were in the line can leave damage behind.

What happens during the inspection

  1. 1
    Find access. Usually the outdoor cleanout. If there isn't one, a toilet can be pulled or a roof vent used.
  2. 2
    Run the camera. The camera head is pushed down the line slowly while it records, with a distance counter on screen.
  3. 3
    Locate problems from above. A small transmitter in the camera head lets us find its exact position and depth from the yard, then mark it.
  4. 4
    Walk through the video with you. We show you each finding and explain what it means.
  5. 5
    Give you the footage and options. You keep the video and get written findings and repair choices.

Reading the results

Not every finding needs action. The difference between watch it and fix it now is usually obvious on the video.

Sewer camera findings and what they usually call for.
FindingHow seriousTypical next step
Light scale or buildup.Low.Cleaning or jetting.
Fine roots at a joint.Moderate.Cut roots and recheck in a year or two.
Heavy root mass.High.Clear roots and repair or line the joint.
Offset or separated joint.Moderate to high.Spot repair.
Belly holding water.Moderate.Monitor, or dig and re-grade if it causes clogs.
Crack, hole or collapse.High.Repair or replacement.

A single serious finding usually leads to underground sewer line repair. Damage along most of the line points to sewer line replacement instead.

Buying in Gainesville's older neighborhoods

Houses in the Duckpond, Pleasant Street, the Southeast district and University Heights are some of the most sought-after in the city. Many also have the oldest drain lines. Clay tile laterals with open joints and cast iron that has corroded for decades are both common in homes of that age.

A sewer camera inspection before closing costs little compared with a lateral replacement discovered after move-in. If the video shows trouble, it gives the buyer something concrete to negotiate with. If it shows a healthy line, that's worth knowing too.

The same applies to mid-century homes across the city, where fiber pipe was sometimes used for the lateral. That material is almost always found by camera, since it looks fine from the yard until it fails.

Camera before cleaning, or after?

If the line is fully blocked, the camera can't see past the clog, so the line is opened first with a cable. The camera then shows why it clogged.

For older lines that clog repeatedly, the camera goes in before any aggressive cleaning. It tells us whether high-pressure hydro jetting is safe or whether the pipe is too fragile for it. For a simple first clog, plain drain cleaning may be all that's needed.

What affects the price of a camera inspection

  • $
    Access. An outdoor cleanout is quickest. Pulling a toilet or going through the roof adds time.
  • $
    Line length. Long laterals on large lots take longer to run and record.
  • $
    Clearing first. A blocked line needs opening before the camera can see anything.
  • $
    Locating. Marking problem spots from the surface is included when repairs are likely.

Inspections across the county

We run camera inspections in every Gainesville ZIP and in the towns around it, including High Springs and the other outlying towns. On properties with a septic tank, the camera covers the line from the house to the tank inlet.

Findings are classified by the pipe condition visible on video. Repairs follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).
