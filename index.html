Before the truck arrives

What to do in the first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

An emergency plumber can only get there so fast. What you do before the truck pulls up decides how much drywall, flooring and cabinetry you lose. Work through this list while you make the call.

  1. 1
    Shut off the main water valve. Most Gainesville homes have a shutoff where the supply line enters the house, often near the garage or a front hose bib. If you can't find it, say so on the phone and we'll talk you through it.
  2. 2
    Cut power or gas to the water heater if the tank is leaking. Flip the breaker on an electric unit. On a gas unit, turn the gas valve on the supply line to off.
  3. 3
    Stop using every drain if sewage is coming up. Each flush or shower adds to a main-line backup. Leave the fixtures alone until the line is cleared.
  4. 4
    Get valuables off the wet floor and take photos. Rugs, electronics and anything cardboard go first. Photos of the damage help with any insurance claim later.
  5. 5
    Smell gas? Leave first, then call. Get everyone outside, then call 911 and GRU's gas emergency line from a safe distance. Don't flip switches on the way out.

Then call us and describe what's leaking and where. You'll hear what to expect before anyone is dispatched.

Local ground, local leaks

Why slab leaks run high in Gainesville, by part of town

Alachua County is split by the Cody Scarp, an old marine shoreline. To the northeast, clay of the Hawthorn Group covers the limestone. To the southwest, erosion stripped that clay away, and the Ocala Limestone sits just a few feet under the sand.

Both conditions move pipes, in different ways. Knowing which one sits under your house tells a plumber where to look first, which saves time and saves concrete.

Water and housing

Gainesville water and older homes: what they do to plumbing

Hard water from the Floridan aquifer

GRU draws Gainesville's water from the Floridan aquifer and treats it at the Murphree Water Treatment Plant. The treatment includes lime softening, yet GRU still lists hardness at about 140 mg/L, or 8.2 grains per gallon. That is firmly in the hard range.

At that level, scale builds inside tank water heaters, tankless heat exchangers and faucet cartridges. A tank that hasn't been flushed in years loses capacity and starts to rumble. A water softener sized for 8 grains stops the white crust at the source, and tank water heater repair is where most scale damage ends up.

Historic districts and pre-1950 pipe

The Duckpond, officially the Northeast Gainesville Residential District, covers about 1,660 acres and 229 historic buildings. It joined the National Register in 1980. Pleasant Street, the Southeast district and both University Heights districts hold many homes from the same era.

Houses from that period were commonly plumbed with galvanized steel supply and cast iron or clay drains. Galvanized rusts from the inside, so pressure drops long before it leaks. Brown water after a few days away usually points to the pipe, not the utility, and whole-home repiping in these houses is planned around plaster walls and original trim.

Sources: GRU water quality FAQ (hardness and source), Northeast Gainesville Residential District (National Register listing).

Weather

Storm season and the occasional hard freeze

Flood risk in Gainesville centers on Hogtown Creek on the west side and Sweetwater Branch on the east. When Hurricane Irma came through in September 2017, one rental on NW 34th Street took nearly four feet of water inside.

Heavy rain can push water into cracked sewer laterals and overload the lines. It shows up as a gurgling toilet or a floor drain backing up. If a storm leaves sewage in a tub, our professional drain cleaning service clears the line before the house is used again, and underground sewer line repair handles the crack that let the water in. Soaked floors and walls go to our water damage restoration service, which runs on its own phone line.

Freezes are rare here, but they happen, sometimes as late as March. When the forecast drops into the 20s overnight, drip the faucets on outside walls, cover the hose bibs and disconnect garden hoses. A pipe that splits in an attic or garage wall needs burst pipe repair right away, before the thaw sends water through the ceiling.

  • $
    Access. A leak behind tile or under a slab costs more to reach than one under a sink.

  • $
    Parts. Tank versus tankless, standard versus high-efficiency fixtures, repair kit versus replacement.

On insurance: most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, but not the pipe itself or a slow leak that ran for weeks. Check your own policy before assuming either way.
